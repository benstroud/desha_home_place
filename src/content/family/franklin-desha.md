**Franklin Wharton Desha** (1819–1869) — who went by **"F.W."** — built this house in **1847**,
and it has been in his family ever since.

He was born on **February 28, 1819**, at the [Washington Navy
Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Navy_Yard), the son of
**Capt. Robert Montgomery Desha** (1796–1822) and **Frances Ann Ferebee**
(1797–1839). His father died in New Orleans in 1822, when Franklin was two years old, and his
mother later married **Hartwell Boswell**, [a prominent early
figure](https://www.jstor.org/stable/40023694) of nearby **Batesville**, where
she died in
1839. Boswell had first married **Rachel Harriet Desha**, a sister of Robert Montgomery Desha,
so he was also Franklin's uncle by marriage. Franklin was the grandson of **Joseph Desha**, the
ninth governor of Kentucky, and the nephew of **Benjamin Desha**, for whom **Desha County** is
named.

As a young man Franklin attended Centre College in Kentucky and graduated from the law and literary
department of Transylvania University in Lexington. He was admitted to the Arkansas bar in 1845 and
practiced in Batesville.

He was a veteran of the **Mexican–American War**. He enlisted in Company D, First Arkansas Mounted
Infantry, as a lieutenant, and was promoted to captain on the field at the Battle of Buena Vista
after the captain's death. On **November 18, 1847**, in Batesville, he married **Elizabeth Jett
Searcy**, daughter of **James Searcy** and niece of **Richard Searcy** — the judge for whom
**Searcy** and **Searcy County** are named.

Franklin and Elizabeth had eleven children. Five survived infancy; they are shown in bold below.

- **Robert Montgomery Desha (1848–1897)**
- James Searcy Desha (1850–1851)
- Franklin Wharton Desha (1852–1853)
- Joseph Egner Desha (1854–1855)
- **Mary Jane McGuire Desha (c. 1856–1920)**
- **Benjamin Desha (1858–1931)**
- Jesse Saffold Desha (1860–1861)
- **Stonewall Jackson Desha (1863–1885)**
- Margaret Frances Desha (1864–1864)
- Martha Desha (1866–1866)
- **Elizabeth Jett Desha (1868–1939)**

During the Civil War Franklin was a delegate to Arkansas's secession conventions. He voted against
secession at the first convention and with the majority at the second. He then organized and
commanded **Desha's Battalion** in the **Confederate Army** until he resigned after the Battle of
Shiloh because of ill health. He was wounded at Fitzhugh's Woods in Woodruff County, Arkansas, on
April 1, 1864. The property was the site of a Confederate encampment in 1863; see
[the history](/history) for the family's account and the historical marker near the city of Desha.

The two portraits above show him as a young man and in his later years.

His house — a double-pen dogtrot of exactly the sort brought west by Upper South settlers —
was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1986.

Franklin died on **April 30, 1869**, at his home, and is buried in the family cemetery near the
house he built. All eleven of his children are buried there as well.

The line continues through his daughter **Elizabeth Jett Desha** (1868–1939), who married
W. H. Lester. Their son **Desha Lester** married Pearl Craig of Newark, and their only child,
**Betty Lee Lester**, married **Robert Stroud**. Their children are **Robert Desha Stroud** and
**Mary Carolyn Stroud Vandiver**, the current stewards of the house.
