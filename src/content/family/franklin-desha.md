Franklin Desha built this house in **1847**, and it has been in his family ever since.

He was the son of a Robert Desha who settled at **Helena, Arkansas**, and the grandson of
**Joseph Desha**, the ninth governor of Kentucky. His step-father was **Hartwell Boswell**, a
prominent early figure of nearby **Batesville**. His uncle was **Benjamin Desha**, for whom
**Desha County** is named.

Franklin was a veteran of the **Mexican–American War**, and he married **Elizabeth Searcy**,
daughter of Richard Searcy — the judge for whom **Searcy** and **Searcy County** are named.
During the American Civil War he served in the **Confederate Army**, and in 1863 the property
was the site of a Confederate encampment.

The two portraits above show him as a young man and in his later years.

His house — a double-pen dogtrot of exactly the sort brought west by Upper South settlers —
was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1986.
