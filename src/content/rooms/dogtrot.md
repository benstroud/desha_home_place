## What you're looking at

Between the two enclosed rooms of the house runs an open, roofed passage — the **dogtrot**. It's the defining feature of this house form: two "pens" joined under one roof with the breeze between them.

## Why it matters

In the nineteenth century, the dogtrot was pure genius. Open at both ends, it let the breeze move through the house in the hot Arkansas summers, and it gave the family a shaded work and gathering space that was part indoors, part out. Covered wagons, saddles, tools, and children could all shelter under it.

## What to notice

- The **projecting gabled roof** at the center of the front facade shelters the entry to this breezeway.
- Feel the air move — you're standing where Franklin Desha's family stood nearly 180 years ago.
